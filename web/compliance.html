What this is

Most AI governance is paperwork. A policy says a human reviews the agent's work; a dashboard records that someone clicked approve. Neither stops the agent, and neither survives an auditor asking whether the record was edited afterwards.

MojoMo inverts it. An agent tethered to a person cannot complete a gated action until that person answers. The answer is written to a log that cannot be updated or deleted — enforced by a database trigger, not by policy — and every row is chained to the one before it by hash. Alter any row and the chain breaks from that point forward.

How the evidence works

  • Append-only. UPDATE and DELETE on the event log raise an exception. There is no code path that edits history.
  • Hash-chained. Each event stores the previous event's hash. Tampering is detectable, not merely discouraged.
  • Decision-level. The record is what was decided and by whom — not that an HTTP request arrived.
  • Independently verifiable. The check above is public. You do not have to take our word for it.

Which obligations this speaks to

Article 14 — Human oversight

The overseer must be able to intervene in or stop the system. Here the human is not a reviewer of the agent's output; they are a precondition for it. The action does not happen without them.

Article 19 — Automatically generated logs

Logs kept over the lifetime of the system, retained and available to authorities. Every decision event is recorded with actor, timestamp and content, and cannot be altered after the fact.

Article 26 — Deployer record-keeping

Deployers must keep records of oversight activity. The oversight trail for any single decision can be exported in full, with its hash chain intact.

MojoMo is not a law firm and this is not legal advice. It is a technical control that produces evidence. Whether it satisfies your obligations is a question for your compliance team.
