# Five conversations

Purpose: find out whether anyone will pay for oversight records before we build
more of the product. Not a pitch. A discovery call.

The rule for every call: **do not describe the product until they have described
their problem.** If we talk first we learn nothing.

---

## Who to talk to

Annex III sectors 3, 4 and 5 are the commercial ones. One person from each of
five companies:

1. **Insurtech** — pricing or underwriting model owner. Sector 5.
2. **Credit scoring / lending** — model risk or credit strategy lead. Sector 5.
3. **HR tech** — vendor selling CV screening or candidate ranking. Sector 4.
4. **Enterprise HR** — in-house talent acquisition or people analytics. Sector 4.
5. **Edtech** — admissions or assessment platform. Sector 3.

Target title: Head of AI Governance, Model Risk Manager, Compliance Lead,
Director of Data Science, or CTO at a 50–500 person company. Not a lawyer, not
a policy analyst. Someone who will be asked to prove something to a regulator.

Where to find them: LinkedIn search on the titles above filtered to EU-headquartered
companies in those sectors. Also the AI governance job boards — companies hiring
an AI governance lead right now are companies that have decided this matters.

---

## The five questions

Ask these in order. Shut up after each one.

1. **"When the AI Act high-risk deadline lands, what will you have to show
   someone?"** — Listen for: a document, a log, a dashboard, a shrug.

2. **"Who in your company is on the hook if a model makes a decision nobody
   can explain?"** — Listen for: a named role, or "the team".

3. **"How do you record that a human reviewed a model's output today?"** —
   Listen for: a spreadsheet, a ticket, a Slack thread, nothing.

4. **"If a regulator asked you tomorrow to prove a specific decision had human
   sign-off, how long would it take you to produce that?"** — Listen for: hours,
   days, weeks, "we couldn't".

5. **"What have you already bought or built for this?"** — Listen for: a vendor
   name, an internal tool, or nothing.

Then, only if they ask what we do: one sentence. "We make the human sign-off a
precondition for the action, and keep a log that can't be edited afterwards."

---

## What counts as a yes

- They describe a real, dated obligation they cannot currently meet
- They have tried to solve it and failed, or haven't started and are worried
- They ask what it costs
- They offer to introduce someone else

## What counts as a no

- "Interesting, send me some information" (polite exit)
- They already have a vendor and are happy
- They say the deadline will slip again and they are waiting
- They cannot name who is accountable

## What to record

For each call: sector, title, the answer to Q4 (time to produce evidence), what
they have bought, and whether they asked about price. Five calls is enough to
know whether the problem is real and urgent or real and theoretical.
