EU AI Act — technical conformance mapping

Scope. This maps MojoMo's decision event log to three obligations in

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. It describes what the system does and where the

evidence lives. It is not legal advice and does not assert that any particular

deployment is compliant — that depends on the system, the deployer and the

risk classification.

Verified against: commit ef502b2, production at mojomo.io.

Public integrity endpoint: https://mojomo.io/api/compliance/public/verify

Article 14 — Human oversight

Art 14(1): high-risk AI systems shall be designed so that they can be
effectively overseen by natural persons.
Art 14(4)(d): the overseer must be able to decide not to use the system or
otherwise disregard, override or reverse its output.
Art 14(4)(e): the overseer must be able to intervene in the operation of the
system or stop it.

What MojoMo does. An agent tethered to a human cannot complete a gated

action until that human answers a decision request. The gate is enforced in the

application, not by policy: the action is blocked at the point of execution.

Where the evidence is.

ObligationMechanismEvidence

|---|---|---|

14(1) effective oversightTethering: agent has no valid action path without a named human`agent.agents.tether_user_id` NOT NULL for active agents
14(4)(d) override / disregardHuman answers `no`; the gated task is not approved`agent.decision_requests.answer`, `agent.tasks.human_approved_at`
14(4)(e) intervene / stopHuman can withdraw or decline; agent key can be revoked`agent.decision_events` event=`withdrawn`; `agent.api_keys.revoked_at`

Limitation. MojoMo gates actions the agent routes through the decision API.

It cannot stop an agent that bypasses the API entirely. The control is only as

strong as the integration.

Article 19 — Automatically generated logs

Art 19: providers shall keep automatically generated logs over the lifetime
of the system, retained for a period appropriate to the intended purpose, and
make them available to competent authorities on request.

What MojoMo does. Every decision event is written to

agent.decision_events at the moment it occurs, by the service, without human

action. The table is append-only.

Where the evidence is.

What is logged. Event type (created, notified, answered, expired,

withdrawn), the agent, the human, the actor class, a canonical JSON detail

payload, and a timestamp.

Limitation. Retention period is not configurable per deployment. There is

no automated export to a customer's own storage. Both are straightforward to

add and are not present today.

Article 26 — Obligations of deployers

Art 26(1): deployers shall implement human oversight according to the
instructions of use.
Art 26(6): deployers shall keep the logs automatically generated by the
high-risk AI system for a period appropriate to the intended purpose.

What MojoMo does. The deployer is the organisation whose human is tethered

to the agent. Their oversight activity — every decision they made and when — is

recorded automatically and can be exported per decision.

How the integrity claim is verified

The chain is only meaningful if it can be checked independently. Three checks:

1. Append-only. UPDATE and DELETE on agent.decision_events raise

agent.decision_events is append-only. Verified by attempting both.

2. Tamper detection. Dropping the trigger, altering a row and restoring the

trigger causes verifyChain() to report brokenAt at the altered row.

Verified against production data.

3. Public verification. GET /api/compliance/public/verify returns the

event count, head hash and check time with no authentication and no

disclosure of decision content.

Canonicalisation. Hashes are computed over a canonical JSON form with

recursively sorted keys and ISO-8601 dates. This matters: Postgres jsonb does

not preserve key order, and a Date serialises differently as a live object

than as a stored string. Both were bugs found and fixed during development; the

stored detail_canonical column holds the exact string that was hashed.

What this does not do

  • It does not classify a system's risk under the Act.
  • It does not produce the technical documentation required by Art 11.
  • It does not perform conformity assessment or CE marking.
  • It does not cover Art 12 (record-keeping by design) beyond the decision log.
  • It does not stop an agent that bypasses the decision API.

    • It is one control, producing one class of evidence, verifiably.
