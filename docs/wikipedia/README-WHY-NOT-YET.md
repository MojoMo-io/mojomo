# Wikipedia: why this cannot be published yet

**Short version: MojoMo does not currently meet Wikipedia's notability
requirement, and publishing now would get the article deleted within days.**

This is not a judgement about the project. It is a mechanical reading of the
rules, checked against the live site and the live web on 2026-10-25.

---

## The rule that decides it

Wikipedia's general notability guideline requires **significant coverage in
reliable, independent, secondary sources**. "Independent" means written by
someone with no connection to the subject. "Secondary" means someone else
analysed it, not that we described ourselves.

A topic with no such sources "should not have a separate article."

## What we checked

- **English Wikipedia search for "MojoMo"** — no existing article. The name is
  free, but also unused, which is itself a signal.
- **Web search for MojoMo + AI agents / MCP / tethering** — returns only
  mojomo.io itself, plus an unrelated Dutch branding agency at mojomo.nl and an
  unrelated content service at mojomo.io/book.
- **Hacker News** — zero mentions of us. All "mojomo" hits are unrelated
  projects (MojoMojo wiki, MojoMotor CMS, Mojomox logo maker).
- **No press, no analyst coverage, no third-party write-ups found.**

## What does NOT count as a source

These are all things we control or submitted ourselves. A reviewer will reject
every one:

- mojomo.io and its subpages
- the npm package page
- the MCP registry listing (we submitted it)
- our own GitHub repos
- our own docs, whitepaper, or facts sheet
- anything we wrote and published anywhere

## What would count

Any **one** of these would materially change the picture:

- A technology journalist writing about the agent-tethering model
- Coverage in a developer publication (The Register, Ars Technica, InfoWorld,
  dev.to front page, Hacker News front page)
- An academic paper or industry report citing the platform
- A conference talk write-up by a third party
- Independent analysis of the funnel data we published

## The conflict-of-interest problem, separately

Even once sources exist, Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest guideline says editors
should not write articles about their own products. The correct path is:

1. Create it as a **Draft:** (not a live article) — drafts are not deleted for
   lacking sources the way live articles are.
2. Declare the conflict of interest on the draft's talk page.
3. Submit for review via Articles for Creation.
4. Let an independent reviewer decide.

Writing it directly into mainspace as the founder is the single fastest way to
get it deleted and to get the account flagged.

## What is in this folder

- `DRAFT-MojoMo.md` — a neutral, source-shaped draft. It is written to
  Wikipedia's tone (no marketing language) and every claim is marked with what
  it would need as a citation. It is **not publishable today**; it is a
  scaffold to fill in as coverage arrives.

## The honest recommendation

Do not chase Wikipedia. It is a lagging indicator of coverage you do not have
yet, and a deleted article is worse than no article — it is a public record that
you tried and were rejected.

Get one piece of real third-party coverage first. The most likely route, given
what exists: the funnel data. Almost nobody has published measured
agent-to-human conversion numbers. That is a genuine story a journalist could
write about without needing to trust our marketing.
